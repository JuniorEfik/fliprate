# Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations

## Limitations

FlipRate is presently a planned measurement study, not an executed benchmark. No provider/model calls, pilot or main experiments, confirmatory data collection, completed human adjudication, final prompt lock or empirical results exist. The only quantitative findings are from seeded synthetic operating-characteristic simulations.

The original 80% power target is not met. Estimated single-active-model CR1/Holm power is **72.24% ± 0.63%**. The owner accepted this as a documented limitation while preserving the 15-call tree, 96 confirmatory families, 6,840-call envelope, primary estimand, 0.05 SESOI and CR1/Holm multiplicity control. Any eventual study is therefore lower-powered exploratory; owner acceptance does not make the failed target pass.

The pressure treatment deliberately bundles asserted confidence and authority. It cannot identify their separate effects, and a model may rationally treat social testimony as information even when it adds no task fact. Reported support distributions and selected actions are behavioral outputs, not calibrated probabilities, latent beliefs or proof of irrationality. Turn-2 paths mix current framing with accumulated context and prior assistant responses, so they support bounded history-path descriptions rather than pure memory, recovery or hysteresis claims.

Inference is conditional on the registered two-domain construction frame, template-cluster assumptions, item validity and four-cell completeness. Synthetic variance, ceiling and missingness assumptions are not empirical model/provider measurements. The construction-frame endpoint bootstrap under-covered in a correlated-failure stress test, so endpoint intervals remain descriptive and all-family sign/SESOI claims require exact finite-roster bounds. Generalization is limited to any eventually tested tasks, prompts, model snapshots and dates.

The novelty claim is narrow and search-bounded. Close prior work already studies warranted versus unsupported updating, valid versus invalid arguments, authority, repeated pressure, multi-turn trajectories, confidence revision and prompt confounds. FlipRate may contribute a cleaner combination of underdetermined objective items, sufficiency-by-pressure factorial control and shared-parent serial paths; it does not claim global priority.

Operational feasibility is unresolved. Provider credentials, exact model access, funded budget authority, named reviewers/adjudicator, accepted evidence packages, timed calibration, institutional determinations, parser/replay/storage validation and a final prompt/model lock are absent or unauthorized.

## Dual-use and ethical considerations

Measurements of pressure sensitivity could be used constructively to diagnose brittle updating, improve calibration and design systems that distinguish evidence from rhetoric. The same findings could also help optimize persuasive pressure, authority cues or repetition to manipulate model outputs or human users. Reports should therefore avoid publishing unsupported prescriptions for coercive prompting and should distinguish behavioral associations from mechanisms.

Optimizing directly against FlipRate could produce benchmark gaming: a model might learn to appear stable, refuse reconsideration or follow superficial evidence markers rather than reason better. The benchmark should never reward consistency alone. Any future release should preserve the separate warranted-update, unsupported-yielding, resistance, abstention and malformedness outcomes and document prompt-template sensitivity.

No personal or sensitive participant data have been collected. Future human construction and adjudication may create employment, compensation, confidentiality, conflict-of-interest and data-protection obligations. The applicable institutional determination must be documented before staffing or collection. Reviewer identities and qualifications should be recorded internally without fabricating expertise or exposing unnecessary personal data.

Raw model outputs, prompts, provider metadata and adjudication records should be access-controlled as appropriate, versioned and retained without silent editing. Provider retention and privacy settings remain unverified. Release decisions should consider whether full pressure templates create avoidable misuse risk while retaining enough information for scientific audit.

## Current ethical disposition

The documentation package identifies these risks but does not constitute institutional ethics approval, a data-protection determination or execution authorization.
