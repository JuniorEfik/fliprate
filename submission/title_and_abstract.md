# Title and Abstract

## Project title

**FlipRate: A Lower-Powered Exploratory Protocol for Measuring Evidence Responsiveness Under Social Pressure**

## Abstract

Large language models may revise an answer because new information warrants change, because unsupported social pressure induces yielding, or may resist even decisive evidence. FlipRate is a planned behavioral measurement study designed to separate these cases without claiming access to a model's latent beliefs. Each deliberately underdetermined item pairs valid, relevant evidence that is either sufficient or insufficient to warrant a prespecified target answer with either neutral or bundled authority/confidence pressure framing. A shared-parent 15-call tree yields a primary Turn-1 sufficiency-by-pressure factorial interaction in reported target support, while counterbalanced later paths support secondary repetition and frame-history analyses. The fixed plan contains 96 confirmatory item families across closed-world logic and quantitative reasoning, with three prospective model families and a 6,840-call envelope including development and validation allocations. Primary inference uses template-cluster-robust CR1 tests with Holm multiplicity control and a prespecified 0.05 smallest effect of scientific interest. Seeded synthetic validation estimated affected-model CR1/Holm power at 72.24% ± 0.63%, below the original 80% target; the owner accepted this limitation and classified the design as lower-powered exploratory. The possible contribution is a narrow, search-bounded measurement design rather than a new discovery of selective updating. No provider calls, experiments, confirmatory data collection, completed adjudication, or empirical model results are reported.

**Keywords:** large language models; belief revision; sycophancy; evidence sufficiency; social pressure; behavioral evaluation

## Status note

The abstract describes a planned protocol and synthetic validation only. It is not an empirical-results abstract.
